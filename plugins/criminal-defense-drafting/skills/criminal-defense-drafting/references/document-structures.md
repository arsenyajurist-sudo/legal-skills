# Document structures

Outlines to adapt to the forum's rules, forms, and local practice directions. Mark every rule or authority not supplied by the user `[VERIFY]` or leave a placeholder `[AUTHORITY: …]`. Never invent citations.

## 1. Bail, release, or variation of conditions
1. **Heading:** court, case number, client, the application (new application / variation / review / appeal against refusal).
2. **Relief sought (first):** release on the conditions proposed; or variation of named conditions; or review of detention.
3. **Background:** the charge, the date of arrest and of any earlier decision, the present status, the time in custody, the next hearing.
4. **Legal test:** the statutory or common-law test for bail or detention, with the user's authority `[AUTHORITY: …]`.
5. **Application of the test, risk by risk:** flight (ties, residence, employment, family, passports), interference with witnesses (what is proposed), reoffending (history, plan), danger, and any other statutory ground. For each: the prosecution's basis, why it is weak or answerable, the proposed condition that meets it.
6. **Release plan:** address, surety or sureties (with their means and relationship), reporting, curfew, tagging, travel restrictions, surrender of documents.
7. **Changed circumstances** (for review or variation): what is new since the last decision.
8. **Annexes:** supporting documents (residence, employment, family, medical).
9. **Signature block.**

## 2. Motion to exclude evidence
1. **Heading and relief:** exclude the identified evidence (specify exactly: the item, its source, the volume and page); alternatively limit its use; alternatively request a voir dire or admissibility hearing.
2. **Background:** how the evidence was obtained, by whom, when, under what authority (warrant, consent, statutory power); the chain of events.
3. **Ground(s):** (a) unlawful search or seizure; (b) statement taken without lawful caution, counsel, or interpreter; (c) involuntary statement; (d) hearsay or lack of foundation; (e) unfair prejudice outweighing probative value; (f) breach of disclosure or privilege; (g) derivative evidence. State each ground separately with the rule `[AUTHORITY: …]`.
4. **Application to the facts:** the specific breach, the burden and who carries it, the standard.
5. **Consequence:** why the court should exclude rather than admit with a direction; the effect on fairness of the proceedings.
6. **Alternative relief:** limiting directions, an adverse-weight direction, a stay if the breach is serious.
7. **Evidence relied on:** witnesses to be called on the motion, exhibits.
8. **Signature block.**

## 3. Application to dismiss, discontinue, or stay (abuse of process)
1. **Relief:** dismissal or stay of the charge or the prosecution.
2. **Ground:** the specific abuse (delay, unfair investigation, entrapment, destruction of material, double jeopardy, bad faith, breach of a prior undertaking).
3. **Facts:** chronology with record references.
4. **Legal test and threshold** `[AUTHORITY: …]`: the standard is usually high; show why this case meets it and why a lesser remedy will not cure the unfairness.
5. **Prejudice:** concrete, not general.
6. **Alternative remedies** (exclusion, adverse inference, directions).
7. **Signature block.**

## 4. Disclosure application
1. **Relief:** an order that the prosecution disclose the identified categories by a date; alternatively production by a third party; alternatively directions for a search.
2. **Background:** the charge, the disclosure already made, the gaps; a numbered schedule of requests with dates of requests and responses.
3. **Relevance:** for each category, why it is relevant (undermines the prosecution case, assists the defense, bears on credibility, bears on the legality of the investigation).
4. **Legal basis:** the regime and the standard `[AUTHORITY: …]`.
5. **Proportionality:** a short, specific description of the search or production that is proportionate.
6. **Privilege and sensitivity:** how the defense proposes to protect confidential material.
7. **Consequences of non-compliance:** reserve the right to apply for a stay or exclusion.
8. **Signature block.**

## 5. Written submissions or skeleton argument (merits)
1. **Title and scope.**
2. **Summary of the defense position** in one paragraph.
3. **The charge and the elements** the prosecution must prove.
4. **The issues in the case.**
5. **The evidence on each issue:** a section per issue, with record references and a short assessment of the strength.
6. **Argument per issue:** (a) the prosecution's best point; (b) the defense answer; (c) the inference the court can safely draw.
7. **Law:** the legal framework and authorities `[AUTHORITY: …]`.
8. **Conclusion and relief:** acquittal or the other outcome sought.
9. **Appendix:** chronology; table of authorities; list of exhibits.

## 6. Grounds of appeal (against conviction or sentence)
1. **Heading, the decision under appeal, and the date.**
2. **Grounds, numbered.** For each ground: (a) the error (specific); (b) the category (law, fact, procedure, evidence, direction, sentence); (c) the standard of review; (d) **preservation**: where the point was raised below, or why it is not waived; (e) the record reference; (f) why the error mattered to the result; (g) the relief.
3. **Order:** structural and legal errors first; evidential rulings; findings of fact; sentence.
4. **Fresh evidence:** a separate section if any, with the conditions to admit `[AUTHORITY: …]`.
5. **Relief:** quash the conviction; order a retrial or acquit; or reduce the sentence to a stated figure.
6. **Interim relief:** bail pending appeal if sought.
7. **Annexes:** transcript extracts, rulings, exhibits.

## 7. Response to a prosecution appeal
1. **Position:** the appeal should be dismissed.
2. **Preliminary points:** permission, time limits, scope.
3. **Ground-by-ground response,** with the standard of review and the record.
4. **Cross-appeal or respondent's notice,** if any.
5. **Costs and ancillary matters.**

## 8. Objection to the transcript or minutes
1. **Heading and the record concerned.**
2. **The corrections sought:** a table: page and line | recorded text | proposed correction | basis (audio, notes, witnesses).
3. **Why it matters:** the effect on the issues or the appeal.
4. **Relief:** an order for correction or for a certified record.

## 9. Complaint about investigative or prosecutorial conduct
1. **Addressee:** the supervisory official or the court, as the system provides.
2. **The conduct complained of,** with dates and persons.
3. **The right or rule breached** `[AUTHORITY: …]`.
4. **The effect on the client.**
5. **Remedy requested:** investigation, correction, exclusion of the resulting material, return of property, release.

## 10. Cross-examination outline
For each witness:
1. **Objective** (the two or three points to gain).
2. **Chapters:** short topic blocks, each of leading questions that can be answered "yes" or "no".
3. **Source of each question:** the document, statement, or exhibit with the record reference, for impeachment.
4. **Risk questions:** which topics open doors; what not to ask.
5. **Exhibits to put and the order.**
6. **Prepared re-examination points** to anticipate.

## 11. Questions and instructions for an expert
1. **The expert's role and duty to the court.**
2. **The materials supplied** (a numbered list).
3. **The questions:** specific, answerable, within the expertise; avoid legal conclusions.
4. **Assumptions to be tested;** alternatives to be considered.
5. **Request for raw data, method, error rates, and independent review.**

## 12. Sentencing or mitigation memorandum
1. **Summary of the position and the sentence sought** (a specific figure or range).
2. **The offence and the culpability,** objectively.
3. **The client:** background, health, family, employment, record, rehabilitation.
4. **Mitigating factors,** each with evidence: remorse, cooperation, compensation, delay, early plea.
5. **The guidelines or range and the arithmetic** under each outcome `[AUTHORITY: …]`.
6. **Alternatives to custody** and conditions proposed.
7. **Credit for time served and for the plea.**
8. **Annexes:** references, reports, evidence of compensation.
