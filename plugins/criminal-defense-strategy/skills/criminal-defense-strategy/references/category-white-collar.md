# Economic offences: fraud, theft and embezzlement, tax, abuse of position

The labels and elements vary; adapt them to the charge in the jurisdiction and mark specifics `[VERIFY]`.

## General defense frame in economic cases
- **Civil or criminal?** The central question in many cases: is this a commercial dispute dressed as a crime? Contract, unpaid debt, failure to perform, and business risk are not crimes without the required mental state and conduct. Gather the civil documents and the history of the relationship.
- **Mental state:** dishonesty, intent to deprive, knowledge. Where the mental state rests on inference, attack the inference.
- **Loss and valuation:** the amount sets the offence level and the sentence. Challenge the method: gross vs net, what was actually received, offsets, what the victim would have paid anyway.
- **Role:** who decided, who knew, who benefited; separate the client from the corporate act.
- **Documents:** build the paper trail early; the prosecution is document-driven.
- **Forensic accounting:** instruct an expert early; test the prosecution's tracing and totals.

## Fraud and deception
- Elements: a false representation or concealment, dishonesty, reliance or inducement where required, gain or loss, causation.
- Lines: no representation; true or arguably true; no reliance or no causal link; no dishonesty (belief in the truth or in the right to act); the victim was informed or sophisticated; a commercial promise that was not performed is not a lie when made.
- Traps: the later conduct used to infer an earlier intent; the client's inconsistent explanations; documents altered later.

## Theft, misappropriation, embezzlement
- Elements: property belonging to another, entrusted or in the client's control, appropriation or conversion, dishonesty or intent.
- Lines: authority or consent to use; claim of right or honest belief; accounting disputes; repayment or intent to repay (not a defense to dishonesty in many systems, but it matters for sentence and for intent evidence).
- Traps: the distinction between theft and breach of trust; characterization of corporate funds and limited authority.

## Tax and revenue offences
- Elements: a false return or evasion, dishonesty, the amount of tax lost.
- Lines: error vs dishonesty; reliance on advisers; the correct computation of the liability; limitation periods; the effect of voluntary disclosure and payment on charging and sentence.
- Traps: parallel civil and criminal proceedings; self-incriminating statements in tax audits.

## Abuse of position, breach of fiduciary duty, commercial abuse
- Elements: a position of trust or authority, an act beyond or contrary to it, a mental state, harm.
- Lines: the act was within authority or a business judgment; disclosure and consent of the principal; no harm; no mental state.
- Traps: a business decision judged with hindsight.

## Custody and release in economic cases
- Emphasize the absence of flight and interference risk; propose financial and reporting conditions; offer to surrender passports; stress documents already preserved.
- Prepare for freezing or restraint of assets and for confiscation proceedings: separate applications and deadlines.

## What to collect from day one
- Contracts, correspondence, bank statements, accounting records, board minutes, audit reports.
- Evidence of the commercial context and of the parties' dealings before the alleged offence.
- Evidence that the loss, if any, was compensated or insured.
- A timeline of authority, knowledge, and decisions.
