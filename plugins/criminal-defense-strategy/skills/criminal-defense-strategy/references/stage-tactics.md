# Tactics by stage

Rules differ by forum; the logic below is what to test against the real rules. Mark specifics `[VERIFY]`.

## Pre-charge investigation
- The client's first rule: no statements without counsel; assert the right to silence and to a lawyer in writing where possible.
- Do not let the client "explain it away". A voluntary interview is rarely an advantage.
- Preserve evidence of the client's account (privileged notes), and identify witnesses and records that may vanish.
- Respond to requests for documents or data with counsel: privilege, scope, and self-incrimination issues.
- Consider approaching the prosecutor early, but only with a clear objective and without waiving rights.

## Arrest and the first day
- Check lawfulness of the arrest and the basis for detention; record times, warnings, and who was present.
- Insist on access to counsel before any interview; attend the interview; ensure it is recorded.
- Do not allow ad hoc statements, consent to searches, or the giving of passwords and devices without advice.
- Note any injuries, requests for medical care, interpreter issues.

## Bail, release, and custody
- The question is risk: flight, interference, reoffending, danger. Prepare a specific release plan: surety, residence, reporting, tagging, passports, third-party guarantees.
- Prepare evidence on ties, employment, health, family: not just argument.
- Know the custody time limits and the review dates; apply for review when circumstances change.
- A refusal can be appealed or reviewed: make the record for it.

## Investigation and charging
- Track what the prosecution is building and what it lacks; do not educate it.
- Request disclosure early and keep a log of requests and answers.
- Challenge the charge before it hardens: prepare submissions on the charging decision or the committal or preliminary hearing, where available.
- Consider whether to approach for charge negotiation or a non-conviction disposition: with the price calculated.

## Disclosure
- Know the regime (standard disclosure, schedules of unused material, the prosecution's duty to disclose exculpatory or impeaching material): `[VERIFY: your forum's regime]`.
- Send targeted, reasoned requests; escalate by application to the court when refused; ask for specific orders and note every non-compliance.
- Lost or destroyed material can support an abuse-of-process or stay application or an adverse inference: preserve the record.

## Pre-trial
- Decide the line of defense and its fallback; check them for consistency.
- File the pre-trial applications: exclusion of evidence, severance, bad-character or similar-fact challenges, abuse of process, change of venue, witness issues.
- Prepare the defense case: witnesses, experts, exhibits, the defendant's decision whether to testify (the client's).
- Jury or judge-alone: the choice, where available, is the client's after advice.

## Trial
- Opening: a one-sentence theory of the case.
- Cross-examination: only what you need; leading questions; prove a contradiction with the document, not with rhetoric; never ask the question whose answer you do not know unless the damage is contained.
- **Objections for the record:** raise promptly, in form, and ask for a ruling; otherwise error is not preserved.
- Submissions of no case to answer, directed verdict, or equivalent at the close of the prosecution's case: prepare them.
- Closing: the story, the holes, the standard of proof, the instructions the judge will give.

## Sentencing
- Prepare the mitigation file early: reports, references, compensation, remorse, medical.
- Calculate the sentence under each outcome and ask for a specific figure, not a general plea for leniency.
- Challenge the prosecution's aggravating factors and the factual basis of sentence.
- Ask for credit for time served and for the plea.

## After conviction
- Calendar the appeal deadline the day of the verdict.
- Order the transcript.
- Identify preserved issues and unpreserved ones, and what can still be cured.
- Seek bail pending appeal where available.
- Consider the client's decision about appealing, with the risk of a worse outcome explained.

## Enforcement of the sentence
- Early release, parole, remission, transfer, and review rights; prison complaints procedures; deadlines to apply.
- Compensation and confiscation orders: challenge the basis and the amount.
