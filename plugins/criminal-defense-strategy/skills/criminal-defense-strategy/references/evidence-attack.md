# Attacking the evidence

Rules of evidence differ by system; test everything against the real rules and mark specifics `[VERIFY]`.

## Asymmetry of admissibility
The prosecution must establish admissibility and sufficiency of each piece of evidence; the defense needs only reasonable doubt. Attack admissibility first when a hole is structural; attack reliability when the evidence will come in anyway. An exclusion that removes the pillar is worth more than ten points of credibility.

## Direct grounds for exclusion (where the system provides them)
- Unlawful search, seizure, or interception; defective warrant; evidence found as the result of an unlawful step.
- Statements taken without lawful caution, access to a lawyer, or an interpreter; coerced or involuntary statements.
- Hearsay and its exceptions; character and similar-fact evidence; opinion evidence outside expertise.
- Unfair prejudice that outweighs the probative value.
- Privilege and confidentiality (legal advice, litigation privilege, spousal or other protected communications).
- Late or non-disclosed evidence; breach of disclosure duties.

## Typical defects by type of evidence
- **Witness testimony:** no personal knowledge, interest or bias, prior inconsistent statements, suggestive identification procedures, passage of time, collusion between witnesses.
- **Complainant:** motive to lie, civil dispute behind the complaint, changes of account, inconsistency with documents.
- **Confessions:** voluntariness, recording, caution, the interviewer's conduct, vulnerability of the suspect, mismatch with the physical evidence.
- **Documents:** authenticity, authorship, provenance, copies vs originals, later additions.
- **Experts:** qualifications, independence, method and its acceptance, the questions put, the materials supplied, error rates, chain of custody, scope beyond expertise; challenge by cross-examination, a counter-expert, or a request for the raw data.
- **Digital evidence:** source, extraction, hashing, completeness, authentication, who could have altered it, metadata, time zones.
- **Covert surveillance, informants, undercover operations:** authorization and its scope, who initiated the contact, entrapment, informants' payments and deals, disclosure and public-interest-immunity or equivalent claims.
- **Forensics (DNA, fingerprints, ballistics, drugs):** contamination, interpretation of mixtures, lab accreditation and validity.

## How to raise it
- Raise it in time and in the proper form: a pre-trial application, an objection at the point of tender, a motion to exclude. An issue not raised is usually waived on appeal.
- Ask for the ruling on the record, with reasons.
- If the court admits it, keep attacking weight: cross-examination, expert response, jury directions.
- Make an offer of proof for evidence the court refuses to admit.

## Defense investigation
- Preserve the scene and data early: letters of preservation, requests to third parties, defense site visits with photographs and measurements.
- Interview witnesses with care (who attends, how it is recorded, ethical limits): never induce testimony.
- Instruct experts early; define the questions; keep the instruction privileged until the report is served.
- Obtain records: communications data, CCTV, medical, financial, employment records.
- Subpoenas and third-party production for non-party material, where available.
