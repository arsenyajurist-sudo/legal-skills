# Bribery and public-office offences

Statutes and elements vary widely by country (for example bribery of officials, foreign bribery, misconduct in public office, abuse of authority). Adapt to the charge and mark specifics `[VERIFY]`.

## First question: is the client a public official or a private party?
Status determines the offence, the mental state, the defenses, and the penalty. Check who counts as a public official, which acts count as official acts, and whether the person acted within their functions.

## Taking a bribe (public official side)
- Elements: an advantage, given or promised, in connection with an official act or omission, with the required corrupt intent.
- Lines: no official act or no connection to one; the payment was lawful (a gift under a threshold, a loan, hospitality, a commercial transaction); no agreement or understanding; the advantage went to a third party without the official's knowledge; the client lacked the power the prosecution assigns to them.
- Traps: pattern evidence (regular payments); informants' recordings; lifestyle and unexplained wealth evidence.

## Giving a bribe, intermediation, minor bribery
- Elements: giving or offering an advantage to influence an official act.
- Lines: duress or extortion by the official (where the law gives a defense or mitigation); no intent to influence; the payment was a lawful fee; spontaneous reporting or cooperation as a basis for non-prosecution or mitigation `[VERIFY: your system]`.
- Traps: the line between an intermediary and a co-principal.

## Entrapment: a central line in corruption cases
Corruption cases often rest on undercover operations or informants. Test:
- Who initiated and who pushed: did the authorities induce an offence the client was not otherwise disposed to commit?
- Was there a basis for the operation (prior information) and proper authorization and supervision?
- Did the informant act under pressure, incentive, or payment?
- Was the operation recorded completely, with the instructions and the contact log?
- Remedies: stay or dismissal for abuse of process, exclusion of evidence, or mitigation (depending on the system).
Disclosure of the operation file is the key request: authorization, instructions, informant arrangements, recordings, contact reports.

## Misconduct in office, abuse of authority, exceeding powers
- Elements: a public function, an act beyond or contrary to it, serious harm or abuse, intent or recklessness.
- Lines: the act was within discretion; no serious harm; good-faith policy judgments; lack of the required threshold of seriousness.
- Traps: political or institutional context; collective decisions and individual responsibility.

## What to collect from day one
- The client's position description, powers, delegations, and instructions.
- The decision records and the chain of approvals.
- The financial history of the alleged advantage and of the relationships.
- Any evidence of the undercover operation and how the contact started.
- Disclosure requests and a log of what is missing.
