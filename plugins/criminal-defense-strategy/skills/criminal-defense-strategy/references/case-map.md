# Case map: how to read the case before strategy

All rules are jurisdiction-specific: apply the real forum's rules and mark anything from memory `[VERIFY]`.

## Step 1. Write out the charge exactly as pleaded
Copy the charging language word for word: the statute, the date, the place, the acts alleged, the mental state alleged, the harm or value alleged, the mode of participation (principal, accomplice, conspirator). Note every place where the charging document, the indictment or information, the disclosure, and the witness statements use different words for the same thing: a shifting charge is a defect and an opportunity.

## Step 2. Break the offence into elements
List each element the prosecution must prove: the conduct, the mental state, the circumstances, causation, the result, jurisdictional facts, aggravating factors that raise the penalty. For each element write the standard (beyond reasonable doubt or the local equivalent) and who carries the burden. Do not forget elements that look procedural but are in fact elements (value thresholds, official status, lack of consent).

## Step 3. Build the evidence table
| Element | Prosecution evidence | Type | Source (volume, page) | Admissible? | Reliable? | Defense attack |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
Types: witness statements, documents, forensic and expert reports, digital evidence, recordings, undercover or informant evidence, confessions, physical items. One row per piece of evidence per element. A piece of evidence that carries several elements gets several rows.

## Step 4. Find the holes
- An element with no evidence, or only with one weak piece.
- Evidence obtained or recorded in breach of rules (unlawful search, no rights advisement, unrecorded interview, chain of custody gaps).
- Contradictions between witnesses, between a witness and a document, between the expert and the file.
- Gaps in time and location (who was where, when).
- Late changes in the prosecution's theory.
- Missing disclosure: what should exist but is not in the file (recordings, notes, communications data, forensic bench notes, informant payments).
- Weaknesses in the complainant's credibility: motive, prior inconsistent statements, interest in a civil claim.

## Step 5. Checklist for reading the file
- **Charging document:** clear and specific? duplicity? the date range?
- **Rights and custody:** time and manner of arrest, access to a lawyer, interview recordings, caution or advisement, interpreter.
- **Searches and seizures:** warrants (scope, date, signature), the inventory, who was present, items seized beyond the warrant.
- **Interviews and statements:** recorded? verbatim? who asked what? signs of pressure, leading questions?
- **Expert reports:** qualifications, method, the questions asked, the materials supplied, the margin of error, chain of custody.
- **Digital evidence:** source, extraction method, hash values, completeness, who had access.
- **Disclosure schedule:** what is listed, what is redacted, what is claimed as sensitive; what the defense has requested.
- **Timeline:** every date and deadline, including custody time limits and speedy-trial or time-limit rules.

## Step 6. Defense resources
List what the defense actually has: witnesses it can call, documents, alibi material, character evidence, expert availability, funding, the client's own account (privileged), time. A line of defense that needs a resource the defense does not have is not yet a line.
