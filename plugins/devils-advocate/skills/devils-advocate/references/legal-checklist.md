# Devil's Advocate: legal checklist

An extended list of questions for a critical review of a legal position. Use it as a reference: go through the relevant sections and look for where the user's position is vulnerable.

This checklist is a jurisdiction-neutral framework. Apply the rules of the actual jurisdiction, forum, and procedure. Mark any rule or authority you cite from memory with `[VERIFY]`; never invent citations.

## 1. Procedural holes

### Time limits
- Has the limitation period run? General or special period?
- From when does the period run: the date of the breach, or the date the claimant knew or should have known?
- Was the period interrupted or restarted (acknowledgment of debt, part payment)? Can you prove it?
- Are procedural deadlines missed for appeals, filing evidence, motions?
- If a deadline is missed: is there good cause, and what proves it?

### Jurisdiction and venue
- Is this the right court or tribunal (civil, commercial, administrative, arbitration)?
- Territorial jurisdiction: is there a forum-selection clause that changes the rules?
- Does the dispute fall under exclusive jurisdiction (real estate, inheritance)?
- Is the dispute subject to arbitration under the parties' agreement?

### Proper parties
- Is this the right claimant: do they have the right to demand? (especially after assignment, succession, collective actions)
- Is this the right respondent: should a co-respondent be joined (joint, secondary liability)?
- Are all interested third parties joined (those whose rights are affected)?

### Formalities
- Are mandatory pre-action steps satisfied (where required: contract, tax, consumer disputes)?
- Are court fees calculated and paid correctly?
- Representative's power of attorney: not expired, not revoked, does it cover specific acts (admitting the claim, withdrawing, settling)?
- Are all mandatory documents attached?

## 2. Substantive holes

### Characterization of the relationship
- Is the nature of the dispute right: contract, tort, unjust enrichment?
- Is the type of contract right, or is an employment contract disguised as a service contract, or a construction contract as services?
- Are the elements of the wrong right (for tort, for liability)?
- Is there an alternative characterization that is better for the opponent?

### Cause of action and relief
- Is the cause of action clear (the facts relied on)?
- Is the relief sought clear (what exactly are you demanding)?
- Are several grounds mixed in a way the opponent can separate and attack one by one?

### Rules of law
- Is the citation of rules exact? Was a different version in force at the time of the events?
- Is there a specific rule that overrides the general one and works against you?
- How do courts read this rule now: has the practice changed?

### Causation (for damages and torts)
- Do you prove direct causation or only indirect?
- Are there intervening causes that break the chain?
- Could the loss have arisen from other causes?

### Size of the claim
- Is the amount justified arithmetically: what supports each figure?
- Is it justified under the governing rules: which method was used?
- Are moral damages, lost profits, interest inflated?
- Can the court reduce penalties or liquidated damages where the law allows it?

## 3. Evidentiary holes

### For each key piece of evidence ask
- **Admissible?** Obtained in breach of law, from an improper source?
- **Relevant?** Does it prove the disputed fact, or something adjacent?
- **Reliable?** Can authenticity, authorship, date, or method be challenged?
- **Sufficient?** Is this evidence alone enough, or is a body of evidence needed?

### Typical attacks on evidence
- Documents: forged signature, later additions, original or copy, notarization
- Witnesses: interest, contradictions with other evidence, no personal knowledge, age of events and human memory
- Experts: qualifications, method, limits of competence, possibility of a repeat or combined expert examination
- Electronic evidence: chain of custody, metadata, notarized or certified capture
- Audio and video: legality of obtaining (especially covert recording), editing, voice identification

### Burden of proof
- Is it really on the opponent, not on you? Will the court reverse the presumption?
- Where do special presumptions apply (carrier's fault, seller's fault, innocence)?

## 4. Tactical holes

### Internal consistency
- Does the claim contradict itself in different parts?
- Do your current explanations contradict what the client wrote or said earlier (pre-action correspondence, records, social media)?
- Are there mismatches between dates, amounts, descriptions of events?

### The client as a source of risk
- What will the client say in cross-examination that sinks the position?
- Does the client have background information the opponent will dig up (past similar conflicts, convictions, dismissals)?
- Will the client give an emotional reaction that turns the court against them?

### Case law
- What does the latest case law from the highest court say on your issue: for you or against?
- What do recent decisions of the intermediate courts say?
- Is there a published review of practice that directly hits your position?
- Can the opponent bring their own bundle of authority, more recent and more relevant?

### Will it fall apart in cross-examination
- Which question, put to the client or a witness, will bring the position down?
- Which document, produced at the right moment, will devalue the argument?

## 5. Risks outside the proceeding

- **Reputational:** is the client ready for publicity and for unpleasant facts being aired?
- **Tax:** if you win, does a tax liability arise on the amount received?
- **Regulatory:** will the case draw the attention of supervisory bodies in a way that hurts the client?
- **Counter-risks:** will the opponent get a counterclaim, a counter-application, a complaint to a regulator or prosecutor?
- **Enforceability:** does the opponent have assets? Have they been moved? Can the judgment be enforced?
- **Economics of the case:** cost vs gain: is settling cheaper?
- **Precedent risk:** will losing create a practice that hurts the client in other cases?

## 6. Authority and precedent (the case-law audit)

Case law decides outcomes, especially in common-law systems. For every authority the user relies on, and every authority the opponent will bring, run this protocol. Never invent a case; mark anything cited from memory `[VERIFY]`.

- **Is it real?** Does the citation resolve to an actual decision (court, date, reporter or neutral citation)? An unverifiable citation is worth nothing and a gift to the opponent.
- **Is it good law?** Subsequent history: overruled, reversed, vacated, superseded by statute, limited, or criticized? Check a citator (for example KeyCite, Shepard's, BAILII, a court's own database), not memory.
- **Binding or merely persuasive here?** Court hierarchy and jurisdiction: is the deciding court above the forum in the same system, or a sister court, a foreign court, or a lower court? Binding authority beats persuasive authority every time.
- **Ratio or dicta?** Is the cited statement the holding (ratio decidendi) or an aside (obiter dictum)? Opponents win by showing the quoted line was dicta.
- **Distinguishable on the facts?** What facts drove the result? List the ways the opponent will distinguish your case, and the ways you can distinguish theirs.
- **Does the facts-to-holding fit survive a close read?** Read the actual passage, not the headnote or a secondary summary.
- **Standard applied.** Which standard of proof, review, or scrutiny did that court apply? Is it the same as here?
- **Counter-authority.** What is the best case against you at the same or higher level? If you have not found it, the opponent will.
- **Direction of travel.** Is the line of cases expanding or shrinking? Is there a pending appeal or legislative change?
- **Statutory interpretation.** Plain text, canons, legislative history: which approach does this court actually use?

## 7. Procedural defects and preservation

Look for procedural violations on both sides, and for the ones that will bind you.

- **Preservation of error.** Was the point raised in time and in the proper form below? An issue not preserved may be waived on appeal. List every argument that is at risk of being waived.
- **Standard of review.** On appeal, is the issue reviewed de novo, for abuse of discretion, or for clear error? The standard often decides the result before the merits.
- **Rules of procedure.** Service, pleading requirements, amendment deadlines, disclosure and discovery obligations, expert-disclosure deadlines, motion deadlines. Which rule did the other side break, and which did you?
- **Sanctions and costs.** Exposure to sanctions, adverse costs, or fee-shifting for conduct or failed positions.
- **Evidence rules.** Hearsay, authentication, privilege and confidentiality (including work product), best-evidence rule, expert admissibility standards.
- **Notice and fair hearing.** Was each party given proper notice and a fair chance to respond? Was anything decided on grounds not argued?
- **Record.** Is the record complete? Is the transcript or minutes accurate? Are objections and rulings on the record?
- **Interlocutory vs final.** Is the order appealable now, or only after final judgment? Is permission required?
- **Jurisdictional defects.** Subject-matter jurisdiction, personal jurisdiction, venue, standing: these cannot be waived and can kill the whole case at any stage.
- **Timing traps.** Deadlines that run from the date of the order, service, or entry, not from when you learned of it.

## 8. Anchor phrases for the review

Not templates (templates are bad), but anchors for stating the attack clearly:

- "This rests on [X]. The opponent will take [X] out with the first motion, expert report, or admissibility challenge."
- "The court will ask: [specific question]. You have no answer."
- "You rely on [rule A]. The opponent will apply [rule B], which is specific and overrides yours." `[VERIFY]`
- "Recent case law from the highest court (judgment of [date], no. [number]) says directly: [thesis]. That is against you." `[VERIFY]`
- "In cross-examination [the witness / the client] will say [X]. After that the rest of the claim does not matter."
- "The limitation period expired on [date]. The opponent will raise it at the first hearing, and the case will be dismissed without reaching the merits."
- "You cite [case] for [proposition]. That line is dicta: the holding turned on [fact] which you do not have." `[VERIFY]`
- "[Case] was overruled or limited by [later authority] in [year]. Relying on it will cost you credibility." `[VERIFY]`
- "That authority is from a court that does not bind this forum. The opponent will call it persuasive at best."
- "You did not raise this point below. On appeal it is waived."
