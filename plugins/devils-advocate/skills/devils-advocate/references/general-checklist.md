# Devil's Advocate: general checklist

For a critical review of ideas, decisions, business plans, texts, strategies, arguments. Run the idea through the relevant sections: wherever it sags, that is where the weak spot lies.

## 1. Logical holes

### Reasoning errors
- **Hidden assumptions.** Which unspoken claims must be true for the argument to hold? Are they all true?
- **Thesis substitution.** They start proving one thing and prove another.
- **False dichotomy.** "Either A or B": are there really only two options?
- **Survivorship bias.** They rely on examples of those who reached the finish, ignoring those who dropped out.
- **Post hoc / correlation is not causation.** "After this" does not mean "because of this".
- **Generalization from the particular.** One or two examples presented as a rule.
- **Circularity.** The argument rests on its own conclusion.
- **Appeal to authority.** "X said so": does X actually understand the matter?

### Internal contradictions
- Do different parts of the argument contradict each other?
- Do the user's current statements contradict what they said earlier (in the same conversation, in past documents)?
- Do the numbers in one part fail to match the numbers in another?

## 2. Factual holes

- **Sources.** Where do the figures and claims come from? Are the sources reliable? Outdated?
- **Numbers.** Do they add up? Rounded so that the picture is distorted?
- **Representativeness of data.** Does the sample reflect reality, or is it cherry-picking?
- **Interpretation.** Are borderline data passed off as an unambiguous result?
- **Definitions.** Are all key terms understood the same way? Is the user relying on vagueness (for example, what counts as "success")?

## 3. Realism of the plan

### Assumptions
- Which 3–5 key assumptions underlie it? How likely is each?
- What happens if at least one fails?
- Is there an assumption whose failure kills everything else?

### Resources
- **Time.** Understated? How long does such a task really take (by historical data of similar projects)?
- **Money.** Are all costs counted: direct, indirect, hidden, opportunity?
- **People.** Are there people of the required qualification? Available? At what cost?
- **Attention.** Will the people executing have the focus if other tasks run in parallel?

### Dependencies
- What in the plan depends on external factors (market, regulation, suppliers, weather, partners)?
- What in the plan requires someone else to do Y, and why will that someone do it?
- Is there a critical path on which any delay shifts everything?

### Plan B
- What is done if the key assumption fails?
- Are there go / no-go decision points?
- What is the loss ceiling beyond which the plan is shut down?

## 4. Competition and resistance

### Who loses if the plan works
- Every change creates losers. Who loses here?
- Do the losers have resources and levers? (political influence, legal tools, market power)
- How exactly will they react?

### Who is already doing this
- Who offers the same thing? Why are they succeeding or failing so far?
- What is your advantage over them: real or imagined?
- If the idea seems obvious: why was it not implemented before you? (Either it is not as obvious as it looks, or someone tried and failed: find out why.)

### Regulation and institutions
- Does the plan run into existing rules? How are they circumvented now, and how will the loopholes be closed?
- Will regulation change in the next 1–3 years in a way that makes the plan illegal?

## 5. Second-order consequences

- **Unintended incentives.** What will people do to circumvent or exploit your solution?
- **Adaptation of the environment.** How will the environment rearrange itself around your solution, and will that rearrangement cancel the point of the solution?
- **Backfire.** Could the solution in the long run strengthen the very problem it is meant to solve?
- **Reverse case.** Imagine the solution is applied not by you but against you. Do you like the picture?

## 6. Success criteria

- How exactly is success measured? By a number, a date, an outside observer?
- Can these criteria be faked or inflated without a real result?
- Is there a deadline after which "if this has not happened, we admit failure"?
- Is "problem solved" being replaced with "actions completed"?

## 7. Specific to texts and argumentation

If the user asks to criticize a text, post, essay, or argument:

- **Main thesis.** Is it clear or blurred? Does it survive a one-sentence summary?
- **Structure.** Does every block work for the thesis? Any padding or digressions?
- **Evidence.** What supports each point? Any unsupported assertions?
- **Counterarguments.** Did the author address the strongest objections, or only the weak ones?
- **Examples.** Exact and apt, or picked for convenience?
- **Conclusions.** Do the conclusions follow from what was said? Is there a leap?
- **Audience.** Will this convince a skeptic, or only the already convinced?

## 8. Specific to business ideas

- **The customer's job.** What real problem does the product solve? Is it invented?
- **Willingness to pay.** Is there evidence of paying demand, or only "that would be cool"?
- **Unit economics.** Customer acquisition cost vs lifetime value: do they add up?
- **Break-even.** When and at what volume? Achievable?
- **Scalability.** What breaks at 10x growth?
- **Defensibility.** What stops a competitor from copying it within a quarter?
- **Team.** Are there people on the team able to execute exactly this?
- **Exit.** Who is the buyer if the exit is a sale? Who wants this revenue if the exit is an IPO?

## 9. Anchor phrases for the review

- "The key assumption here is [X]. The probability that it fails is [estimate]. If it fails, [what collapses]."
- "This argument rests on [X]. It is enough to object [Y] and it stops working."
- "You compare against [A], but the honest comparison is [B]. On that comparison the plan loses."
- "[Who] already tried to solve this in [when]. They failed because [reason]. What is different about you?"
- "If tomorrow [a competitor / the regulator / the market] does [X], your whole plan resets to zero. You have no plan B for that."
- "You measure success by [metric]. That metric is easy to inflate without solving the problem. So you will not know whether it works."
