# Templates

Neutral models. Replace bracketed items; mark anything uncertain `[VERIFY]`. Never invent a rule, a period, or a fee.

## 1. Short reply
**Subject:** [matter]: what we found and what to do next

Dear [name],

You asked about [question]. In short: [one-sentence conclusion].

What this means for you: [two or three sentences in plain words].

What we recommend: [the main step and why]. The alternatives are [A] and [B]; the main risk of [A] is [risk].

The next step is [action] by [date or event]. [Who does what.]

If anything changes, or if you remember something that is not in the documents you gave us, tell us before [date], because it could change the advice.

Kind regards,
[lawyer]

## 2. Step-by-step memo
**What this memo covers:** [one sentence]
**Key deadline:** **[date or period] from [trigger]** `[VERIFY]`

**Step 1. [Action]**
Why: [one line]. Documents: [list and where to get each]. Cost: [amount or "to be confirmed"].

**Step 2. [Action]** …

**Costs overview**
| Item | Amount | Paid to |
|---|---|---|

**Where to go:** [address or website, supplied by the lawyer].

**What can go wrong:** [two or three real risks and what to do if they happen].

## 3. FAQ
**Q1. [The question as the client would ask it]**
A: [2–4 sentences]. *Watch out:* [one point]

(5–10 questions, ordered from the most common to the least.)

## 4. Explanation of a decision
**What the court decided:** [one or two sentences]
**What it means for you:** [plain words]
**Your options now**
1. Comply: [what, by when, consequence].
2. Challenge: [where, by when, the realistic chance, the cost, the risk of a worse outcome].
3. Settle: [what is possible].
**Deadlines:** the time to act runs from [trigger date] `[VERIFY]`. Please tell us the date you received the decision.
**Our advice:** [only if the lawyer has given it].

## Phrases to prefer
- "In plain terms…" / "What this means for you…"
- "There is a risk that…" instead of "this will happen".
- "Based on the documents we have seen…" to signal assumptions.
- "We cannot promise a result, but…" followed by a real assessment.

## Phrases to avoid
- "Don't worry": it dismisses the concern.
- "It is obvious that…" / "clearly": it hides reasoning.
- "You will win": never.
- Latin or technical terms with no definition.
