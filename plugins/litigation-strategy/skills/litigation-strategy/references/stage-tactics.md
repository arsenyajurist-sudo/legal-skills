# Tactics by stage

Rules differ by forum; the logic below is what to test against the real rules. Mark specifics `[VERIFY]`.

## Pre-action stage
- **Fix the facts first:** preservation of documents and electronic data (send a litigation-hold notice), photographs, witness notes, notarized or certified captures of web pages and messages.
- **Letter before action:** content that makes the next step easy for the court: facts, the legal basis, the sum, a short deadline, the consequence (issue proceedings, seek costs). Check any pre-action protocol or contract notice requirement; non-compliance can cost you costs even if you win.
- **Without prejudice or "settlement" communications:** know what is privileged and what is not; do not write an admission by accident.
- **Limitation watch:** diary the date; if close, issue first and negotiate after.
- **Choice of forum and defendant:** where is the respondent, where are the assets, which court gives the best procedure, speed, and enforcement.

## Issuing the claim
- Plead the facts that carry each element and nothing that creates new burdens. A pleading is a document you will be held to.
- **Interim relief with the claim** if there is a real risk of dissipation: freezing or attachment of assets, injunctions, preservation orders. Prepare evidence of risk and the undertaking in damages.
- **Joinder:** all necessary parties; guarantors; insurers where allowed.
- **Quantum:** every figure supportable; separate heads of loss.

## Case preparation (case management)
- **Disclosure or discovery:** ask for specific categories, not fishing. Resist overbroad requests and protect privilege. Know the deadlines and the sanctions for default.
- **Experts:** choose early, define the questions, mind the independence duties and the deadline to serve reports.
- **Witness statements:** check each against the documents; a statement that contradicts a contemporaneous document loses.
- **Summary disposal:** consider an application for summary judgment, strike-out, or dismissal on a point of law when the other side's case has no real prospect, and consider what an application against you would target.
- **Amendments:** closing off alternative pleadings and fixing the issues list; late amendments cost.

## Hearing or trial
- Opening: the one-page theory of the case, the issues, the documents bundle map.
- Cross-examination: only what you need; leading questions; prove the contradiction with the document, not with rhetoric.
- **Objections for the record:** raise promptly, in form, and ask for a ruling; otherwise error is not preserved.
- **Submissions:** the judge's reasoning in advance: offer the court the route to your result and the authorities, including the binding ones against you (and how to distinguish them).

## Default or uncontested judgment
Deadlines to respond or to set aside are short and strict. If you are the respondent: act at once, with a draft defence and the reason for delay. If you are the claimant: check service carefully, as defective service is the standard ground for setting aside.

## Settlement and ADR
Time it for when the other side has seen its weak point (after disclosure or expert reports, before trial costs). Use formal offers where they exist. Draft with enforcement in mind: consent judgment, security, acceleration, confidentiality, tax treatment.

## Enforcement
- Start the tracing early; do not wait for judgment.
- Choose the method to match the asset: garnishment of accounts, charging order on land, attachment of vehicles, receivership, examination of the debtor.
- Watch the limitation of the judgment itself and revival procedures.
- Watch for transactions the debtor makes to avoid enforcement (transactions at an undervalue, fraudulent conveyance, preferences) and for insolvency of the debtor.
