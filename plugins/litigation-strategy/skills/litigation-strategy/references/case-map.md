# Dispute map: method

Goal: in 20 minutes turn "we have a dispute" into a table that shows where the position holds, where it leaks, and what burns by deadline.

All rules are jurisdiction-specific: apply the real forum's rules and mark anything from memory `[VERIFY]`.

## 1. Claim and rule
- State the claim in one line: who, against whom, asks for what (pay / declare / compel / rescind / set aside / apply consequences).
- The substantive rule the claim stands on (statute, case law, contract term). If you cannot name it, the claim is not yet formulated.
- The cause of action or remedy chosen. A wrongly chosen cause of action means losing even when right on the merits (a frequent reason for losses).

## 2. Elements the claimant must prove (with a source for each)
Break the claim into elements as a prosecutor would break a charge. Typical sets:
- **Debt under a contract:** the contract was concluded (form matters), the claimant performed, the respondent's time to perform arrived, the amount, the calculation of penalty or interest, compliance with pre-action requirements.
- **Damages:** breach, loss, causation, fault or the standard of liability, quantum proved with reasonable certainty, mitigation.
- **Unjust enrichment / restitution:** enrichment, at the claimant's expense, without legal basis; the respondent's defences (change of position, passing on, voluntary payment).
- **Invalidity or rescission of a transaction:** the defect (misrepresentation, duress, undue influence, mistake, illegality, incapacity, sham), the claimant's standing, the time limit, the consequences and restitution.
- **Declaration of rights (property or status):** the root of title or right, no dispute about the object itself, the proper respondent.
- **Family or maintenance claims:** the pool of assets and valuation date, needs and means, statutory discretion.
For each element: what proves it (document, volume, page), what does NOT, and whether it can be obtained (disclosure, subpoena, request, expert).

## 3. What the respondent must prove
Defences where the burden is on the respondent: performance, payment, set-off, lien or retention, contributory fault, failure to mitigate, penalty clause unenforceable or disproportionate (where the system allows relief), limitation (a defence that must usually be raised in time).
Presumptions that work against a party: breach and fault presumed in some regimes, special statutory presumptions, rebuttable presumptions about common property, reasonableness and good faith. List which ones apply and who carries each burden.

## 4. Holes and defects
A separate list: an element with no evidence; evidence obtained unlawfully (inadmissible); required form of the transaction not observed (statute of frauds or equivalent); a document only in copy; a witness with no personal knowledge; a gap in the chain of events; an inconsistency between pleading and documents.

## 5. Deadlines: the table of burning dates
| Deadline | Source | Starts from | Expires | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Limitation of the claim | statute / contract | when the claimant knew or should have known | | |
| Pre-action steps | rules or contract | dispatch | | |
| Response to a claim or default | procedural rules | service | | |
| Setting aside a default judgment | procedural rules | notice | | |
| Appeal / permission to appeal | see appeal-map | date of decision or entry | | |
| Costs application | procedural rules | last order | | |
| Enforcement of the judgment | statute | entry of judgment | | |
Rule: a deadline that burns within 10 days goes in the first line of the answer.

## 6. Enforceability
Before the claim, answer in writing: does the respondent have assets? Check public registers (land, vehicles, company filings, insolvency registers, court records of other proceedings), and what can be traced by asset-tracing tools. Is the respondent solvent? Is there a guarantor or security? What recovery tools exist (charging orders, garnishment, attachment, receivership, freezing relief)? If nothing can be collected, say so before the client pays for a win.

## 7. Related proceedings and preclusion
Are there other cases between the same people? Facts established there may bind this case (issue estoppel, res judicata, collateral estoppel `[VERIFY]`). The position taken here must not contradict the position taken elsewhere. Check also whether a claim in another forum could stay or consolidate this one.
