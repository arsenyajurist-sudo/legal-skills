# Lines of position by category of dispute

For each category: what the claimant and the respondent can build, the usual traps, and the price. These are frameworks, not rules: test them against the actual law of the forum and mark specifics `[VERIFY]`.

## 1. Debt, penalties, interest (contract, loan, supply, services)
- **Claimant:** prove the contract and its terms, performance, the date the debt fell due, the calculation. Chase the strongest document (signed acceptance, ledger, written acknowledgment).
- **Respondent:** deny the debt in whole or part (payment, set-off, defective performance), challenge the amount of penalties or interest, limitation, the claimant's standing (assignment defects), pre-action non-compliance.
- **Traps:** penalty clauses that a court may refuse or reduce; acknowledgment of debt interrupting limitation; part payment; compound interest rules.

## 2. Loans between individuals, IOUs
- **Claimant:** the writing; proof of transfer of funds; proof that it was a loan, not a gift or investment; demand and refusal.
- **Respondent:** gift or other basis; repayment (proof!); the document is a forgery or was signed under pressure; the document does not say what the claimant says.
- **Traps:** informal transactions where the form of writing is required; money moved in cash.

## 3. Unjust enrichment / restitution
- **Claimant:** the benefit, at the claimant's expense, without legal basis; tracing; no defence of change of position.
- **Respondent:** a basis (contract, gift, voluntary payment), change of position, the claimant knew there was no obligation, limitation.
- **Trap:** choosing restitution when a contract claim is the proper one.

## 4. Invalid, voidable, or sham transactions
- **Claimant:** the defect and its category; standing; the time limit; restitution; protection of third parties (bona fide purchasers) and registered titles.
- **Respondent:** affirmation, ratification, delay, a bona fide purchaser, the counterparty's own bad faith, the transaction served a real purpose.
- **Traps:** an argument that a transaction was a sham can also expose the client to a claim that it was one all along; check the client's other positions first.

## 5. Damages
- **Claimant:** breach, causation, foreseeability or remoteness, quantum with reasonable certainty, mitigation. For lost profit: readiness to earn it, a model built on the claimant's own data.
- **Respondent:** break causation (intervening cause, counter-breach), challenge quantum (methodology, speculation), failure to mitigate, contractual limits and exclusions, contributory fault.
- **Traps:** penalty and damages overlap; double recovery; limitation by head of loss.

## 6. Disputes with banks, lenders, collectors (borrower side)
- **Borrower:** unfair or unenforceable terms, disclosure and consumer-credit defects, miscalculation of interest and fees, standing of an assignee (chain of title), limitation, proper service of earlier notices.
- **Lender:** a clean paper trail, an accurate statement of account, proof of assignment.
- **Traps:** an admission in correspondence; default judgments; interest running while you argue.

## 7. Real estate, title, land, housing
- **Claimant:** root of title, registration, adverse possession or prescription where available, boundary evidence (survey), equitable interests.
- **Respondent:** better or earlier title, bona fide purchaser, estoppel by acquiescence, limitation.
- **Traps:** missing registered interest; unregistered rights; interim relief (lis pendens or equivalent) both ways.

## 8. Family and property between spouses or partners
- **Applicant:** the pool of assets, contributions, needs, valuation date, disclosure of the other side's finances.
- **Respondent:** non-matrimonial or pre-acquired property, dissipation allegations, valuation challenges.
- **Traps:** full and frank disclosure duties; agreements and their enforceability.

## 9. Employment disputes (employee or employer side)
- **Employee:** the nature of the relationship (employee vs contractor), unlawful termination or discrimination, contractual entitlements, limitation periods that are often very short.
- **Employer:** a fair process and documented reasons, mitigation, contractual terms, settlement agreements.
- **Traps:** short statutory time limits and mandatory early conciliation or pre-claim steps `[VERIFY]`.

## 10. Consumer protection
- **Consumer:** defect or non-conformity, unfair terms, the statutory remedies and shifts of burden, collective actions where available.
- **Trader:** compliance trail, disclosure given, limits of liability, proportionality of remedy.
- **Traps:** statutory minimum rights that cannot be excluded; regulatory attention.

## 11. Liability of directors and controlling persons; recovery beyond the company
- **Claimant:** breach of duty, wrongful or fraudulent trading, veil-piercing where the law allows it, transactions at an undervalue, misapplication of assets.
- **Respondent:** the business judgment defence, reliance on advisers, lack of knowledge, limitation, the company's actual solvency at the time.
- **Traps:** this is slow and cost-heavy; usually only worth it for a large, traceable recovery.

## 12. Enforcement proceedings
- **Judgment creditor:** the right tool for the asset; speed; third-party debt orders; examination of the debtor; attacking disposals.
- **Debtor:** stay of enforcement, instalments, exemptions, setting aside for irregularity, insolvency options.
- **Traps:** enforcement costs; limitation of the judgment; transfers before or after judgment.

## Choosing between lines: quick rules
- The line that needs the least from a document you do not hold is usually the best.
- If the other side's case rests on one witness or one document, the line is to remove that pillar.
- If both lines are strong, pick the one whose loss costs less.
- Always price the line you are NOT choosing.
