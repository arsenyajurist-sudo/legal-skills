# Economics of a dispute

## Decision formula
Expected value of the dispute = (probability of winning × the amount we will actually recover) − (our costs) − (probability of losing × the other side's costs that will be awarded against us) − (cost of time and of the client's attention).
Do the arithmetic aloud. Often it shows that the "right" claim does not pay.

## Costs
- **Court fees:** use the court's own fee calculator, not memory. Appeals have their own rates.
- **Experts:** budget realistically; an expert is paid in advance and recovered from the loser in proportion, depending on the system.
- **Representation costs:** recoverable only within reason in many systems; courts cut them. Keep time records and fee agreements that support the claim for costs.
- **Adverse-costs risk:** in many common-law systems (England and Wales and others) the loser pays a substantial part of the winner's costs; in others (the "American rule") each side bears its own costs unless a statute or contract shifts them. State which regime applies and `[VERIFY]` it.
- **Offers to settle:** formal offers (for example Part 36 offers in England and Wales, Rule 68 offers in US federal courts) can shift costs: use them tactically `[VERIFY: your forum]`.
- **Security for costs and undertakings in damages:** a claimant seeking an injunction may owe damages if it was wrongly granted; a foreign or impecunious claimant may be ordered to provide security for costs.

## Enforceability: check before suing
1. Registers and tracing: land and vehicle registers, company filings, insolvency registers, litigation history, public asset information.
2. If the debtor has nothing: the judgment may be only a basis to start insolvency proceedings against them (thresholds and effects vary).
3. If the debtor is an empty company: consider claims against directors or controlling persons (wrongful trading, breach of duty, veil-piercing where available): a long road, but sometimes the only one.
4. Recovery tools: attachment, garnishment, charging orders, receivers, freezing relief: ask for the interim measure early.

## Alternatives to litigation
- **Settlement or instalments:** saves court fees, time, and the relationship; fix the terms so that default has teeth (consent judgment, acceleration clause, security).
- **ADR:** mediation, early neutral evaluation, expert determination, arbitration where there is an agreement.
- **Assignment of the debt (sale):** if collection is long and expensive.
- **Set-off, novation, accord and satisfaction.**
- **For the debtor:** restructuring with the bank, refinancing, a formal insolvency procedure.

## What to tell the client (structure)
1. **Chances:** high / medium / low, and why: in terms of evidence, not "the judge is good".
2. **Cost:** fees, expert, representation, time (a first-instance case often takes months to years; an appeal adds more).
3. **What we realistically recover:** from whom, from what, when.
4. **What we risk:** the other side's costs, estoppel, counterclaim, reputational effects.
5. **The alternative and its price.**
No promise of outcome. Any contingency or success-fee arrangement must be clear and lawful in the jurisdiction `[VERIFY: professional rules]`.
