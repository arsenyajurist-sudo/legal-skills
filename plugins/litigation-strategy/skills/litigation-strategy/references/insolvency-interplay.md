# Civil disputes at the border with insolvency

Insolvency regimes differ greatly; use this as a checklist of questions, not a statement of law. Mark specifics `[VERIFY]`.

## 1. A claim against a debtor already in an insolvency procedure
- Is there a stay or moratorium on proceedings and enforcement? What is its scope?
- Does the claim have to be lodged in the insolvency (proof of debt) rather than pursued in court?
- Time limits for lodging; the category of the claim (secured, preferential, unsecured, subordinated).
- Who now speaks for the debtor (the officeholder)? Does the officeholder have to be joined?

## 2. Avoidance of transactions by the debtor
- Transactions at an undervalue, preferences, fraudulent or defrauding transfers, transactions with connected persons.
- Look-back periods, who may bring the claim, and what the counterparty can plead (good faith, ordinary course, value given).
- As a creditor: can you push the officeholder to bring the claim, or take assignment of it? As a counterparty: how to defend (value, good faith, no insolvency at the time).

## 3. Excluding assets from the estate
- Exempt property; property held on trust or belonging to third parties; family-home protections; assets of a spouse; security interests.
- Evidence of title that precedes the insolvency; timely objection to inclusion.

## 4. Discharge and its limits
- Which debts survive discharge (fraud, certain penalties, family obligations, student loans in some systems).
- Behavior that blocks discharge: concealment of assets, false statements, transactions to defeat creditors.

## 5. Liability beyond the company
- Wrongful trading, fraudulent trading, breach of director's duty, veil-piercing, disqualification.
- Standing of creditors versus the officeholder; funding the claim; recovery realism.

## 6. Costs and funding in the procedure
- Who pays the officeholder; deposit requirements; creditor funding of litigation; priority of costs.

## 7. Instalments vs insolvency of the debtor
- When the debtor offers instalments or a settlement, compare with the realistic insolvency dividend and the risk that payments received will be clawed back as preferences.

## Strategy cautions
- A position taken in the civil case binds the client in the insolvency and vice versa (preclusion): check both before committing.
- Never advise moving assets, backdating, or concealing: see the red lines in `SKILL.md`.
